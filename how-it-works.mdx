When you deposit into a Concrete Earn vault, you receive vault share tokens in return. From that point, the vault's automation and underlying strategies manage capital allocation, accounting, and withdrawal processing on your behalf.

## The deposit flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect your wallet">
    Visit [app.concrete.xyz/earn](https://app.concrete.xyz/earn) and connect a compatible Web3 wallet. No account registration is required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a vault">
    Browse the available vaults and select one that matches your asset and  preferences. The Earn page displays vaults, their TVL (Total Value Locked) APY, and any rewards. 
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deposit your assets">
    Enter the amount you want to deposit and confirm the transaction. Concrete accepts the vault's underlying asset directly — you do not need to bridge, swap, or interact with any other protocol yourself. Some vaults have the ability to swap and deposit using the Enso service. 
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive ct[Asset] tokens">
    After your deposit is confirmed, you receive `ct[Asset]` shares to your wallet. The exact symbol depends on the specific vault you deposited into. These shares represent your proportional claim on the vault's underlying assets. Their share-to-asset exchange rate rises as the vault earns yield.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Earn yield ">
    Your deposit is allocated to the vault's configured yield strategies — such as lending markets, liquidity pools, or restaking layers — under the rules set at the vault's deployment. As the vault's underlying strategies generate yield, the share-to-asset exchange rate rises and your `ct[Asset]` shares become redeemable for more of the underlying asset. You do not need to claim, compound, or monitor anything.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request a withdrawal">
    When you are ready to exit, submit a withdrawal request. For vaults that use an asynchronous withdrawal queue (the most common configuration), your request enters an epoch-based processing cycle rather than settling instantly. The cadence depends on the vault and is shown in the Earn app, on the vault page. 
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Concrete vaults reward a longer time horizon. Execution costs for entering and exiting yield positions are absorbed by the vault, and compounding requires time to build. Depositors who stay longer benefit from more rebalancing cycles and a greater share of compounded returns.
</Note>

## What happens inside the vault

Once your deposit is in, three layers of the Concrete system work continuously on your behalf.

### Active management

Concrete vaults do not park your capital in a single protocol. The vault engine continuously deploys assets between different yield-generating strategies — shifting toward better opportunities and pulling back from weaker ones as market conditions change. Yield earned in earlier periods is redeployed into new strategies, compounding over time.

### Role-based automation

Concrete vaults separate responsibilities across distinct on-chain roles, each with its own admin role. Operational roles are typically held by Concrete's automation; governance roles are typically held by the vault's partner:

- **Vault Manager** — sets vault parameters, including fees and deposit / withdrawal limits.
- **Strategy Manager** — adds, removes, and toggles the status of strategies attached to the vault.
- **Hook Manager** — configures optional hook contracts (for example, per-user deposit caps or compliance checks).
- **Allocator** — moves capital between the vault and its strategies and sets the deallocation order used to source liquidity for withdrawals.
- **Withdrawal Manager** (async vaults only) — closes and processes withdrawal epochs, and can claim withdrawals on behalf of users.

This separation keeps high-impact configuration changes under governance oversight while letting routine operations run on automation.

### Daily NAV updates

Each day, Concrete's automation calculates the vault's Net Asset Value (NAV) — the total value of everything the vault holds across all positions and strategies, denominated in the vault's underlying asset. This figure feeds the share-to-asset exchange rate that determines how much each `ct[Asset]` share is redeemable for. As NAV rises, each share is worth more of the underlying asset. NAV updates flow through a three-party model — a transaction proposer, an independent signer, and on-chain safeguards in the underlying strategy contract — so that no single party can unilaterally change the vault's reported value.
