A Concrete Vault accepts capital in several ways depending on the token you
hold and the chain you hold it on. The deposit surface adapts to your wallet:
the same vault may present a direct deposit, a wrapped-asset deposit, a
same-chain swap, or a cross-chain route, and the UI hides paths that do not
apply to your selection.

## Direct deposit

The default path. You select the vault's underlying asset, enter an amount,
and confirm. The vault contract holds the asset and issues vault shares to
you. A standard ERC-20 approval is required before the first deposit; later
deposits reuse the existing allowance until it is exhausted.

## Wrapped deposit

When the underlying is a wrapped token (for example WETH) and you hold the
native asset (ETH), the UI offers a single-step "Wrap and Deposit" flow that
wraps then deposits in one action. You pay one approval and one deposit
transaction.

## Swap deposit (same network)

When you hold an unrelated token on the vault's chain, the UI can route
through Enso to swap into the underlying and then deposit. The quote
includes price impact and a gas estimate before you sign. Token availability
is bounded by Enso route coverage for the source token on that network, so
some assets may appear in your wallet without appearing in the deposit
selector.

## Cross-chain deposit

When you hold a token on a chain different from the vault's chain, the UI
routes through Enso and LayerZero. Concrete uses LayerZero as the cross-chain
messaging primitive (see [LayerZero messaging](../cross-chain/layerzero-messaging)
for the protocol-level detail).

Mechanics:

- The source-chain transaction approves and swaps your token into the format
  expected by the destination vault.
- LayerZero carries the message from the source chain to the vault's chain.
- The destination chain receives the assets and deposits them into the
  vault.

What the UI surfaces while a cross-chain deposit is in flight:

- A per-transaction progress timeline that tracks the source-chain
  transaction, the LayerZero message, and the destination-chain receipt.
- The LayerZero fee included in the source-chain quote. The UI applies a
  small safety buffer so transient gas-price movement on the source chain
  does not cause the source transaction to revert before the message
  dispatches.

Token availability for cross-chain deposits is the intersection of two
layers: the CMS configuration for that vault (which tokens Concrete supports
for that vault as cross-chain sources), and Enso route availability for your
specific token and amount. The selector surfaces only routes that satisfy
both.

## Pre-deposit Vaults

A v2 pre-deposit Vault accepts deposits on a source chain before the
destination vault is live on its target chain. The LayerZero claim moves the
position once the destination vault opens (see
[Pre-deposit claim](../cross-chain/pre-deposit-claim)).

## Confirmations the UI requires before submitting

**Mixed-chain ownership confirmation.** When the destination chain differs
from the source chain, you confirm that you control the same address on the
destination chain. This matters because smart-contract wallets are not
guaranteed to exist at the same address on every chain, and shares delivered
to an address you cannot control on the destination chain are not
recoverable from the UI.

**Withdrawal-delay acceptance.** For an Earn V2 vault with a per-epoch
withdrawal cap configured, you accept that the withdrawal delay may extend
beyond the nominal cycle when queued demand exceeds the cap (see
[Withdrawal caps](../withdrawals/caps-concept) and
[Withdrawal overflow](../withdrawals/overflow-concept)). The acceptance is
shown only when an amount is entered and the vault carries a configured
queue threshold.

## Deposit limits

A vault may carry a deposit cap, a minimum deposit amount, or a remaining
headroom value that shrinks as other depositors fill the cap. The UI
surfaces the cap as a progress bar against the vault's underlying asset
total, and validates your input against the minimum and remaining values
before the deposit button enables.

When the deposit amount in underlying terms would exceed the remaining
headroom, the form raises an over-limit error. When the amount sits below
the configured minimum, the form raises an under-limit error. Both checks
operate on the deposit amount in the vault's underlying decimals, even for
Enso routes where the input token has different decimals.

## Permissioned vaults

Vaults with an on-chain deposit allowlist reject deposits from addresses
that are not on the list. The UI mirrors this constraint and shows a
restricted-access state to unauthorised wallets, so you do not sign a
transaction that would revert (see
[Permissioned vault access](../vaults/permissioned-access)).

## Vault state messages

A vault may be bridging, paused, deprecated, or at its deposit limit. Each
state surfaces a message and adjusts the deposit affordance accordingly.
Deposits are not available while a vault is paused or bridging. A deprecated
vault may continue to surface its message as a fallback when no other state
applies.

## Disabled deposits

When a vault disables deposits, the deposit form is hidden or rendered in a
disabled state. Existing positions are unaffected. You can still withdraw,
claim, and read state.

Disabling deposits is the standard mechanism for:

- Soft-closing a vault before deprecation.
- Freezing a vault prior to a migration.
- Responding to a temporary external constraint.

Deposit disable is independent of withdrawal disable. A vault commonly has
deposits disabled while withdrawals remain enabled.
