An epoch is the repeating settlement window an Earn V2 Vault uses to
batch withdraw requests into a single payout. Epochs give every queued
request a predictable cadence: requests submitted into the same epoch
settle together at that epoch's payout point.

Each epoch moves through the same sequence of states.

**Start.** The previous epoch's payout point is also the start of the
next epoch. The new epoch opens for withdraw requests.

**Cutoff.** The cutoff time is defined by the vault's `cutoffCron`
schedule. After cutoff the epoch closes to new requests, and every
request already in the epoch is locked in for the upcoming payout.

**Closed-pending interval.** Between cutoff and payout the epoch is
closed-pending. Requests already in the epoch cannot be added to or
removed from, with the exception of cancellation. See
[Withdrawal cancellation](./cancellation) for the cancellation flow.

**Payout.** The payout time is defined by the vault's `payoutCron`
schedule. At payout the epoch settles on-chain, claims become available,
and the next epoch starts.

The Withdrawal Queue card and the per-request rows display the estimated
cutoff and payout dates for each epoch. These dates compute from the
current epoch's on-chain `startBlockTimestamp` plus the cron schedule,
not from wall-clock time. They align with how the chain advances, so the
displayed timestamp may shift slightly between visits as the chain
settles.

At any moment exactly one epoch is open. Zero or more closed-pending
epochs may sit between cutoff and payout when the schedule produces a
multi-day gap between the two.

Vaults with a configured cap can split a single request across multiple
epochs. Each slice follows its own epoch's lifecycle independently and
shows the cutoff and payout dates of the epoch that will settle it. See
[Withdrawal overflow](./overflow-concept) for how slicing is rendered.
