# Withdrawal caps

A withdrawal cap is the maximum amount an Earn V2 Vault will pay out in a
single epoch. The cap exists so each epoch's payout size is predictable,
and so a single epoch cannot be drained beyond a safe fraction of the
vault's holdings.

The cap is set as a percentage of the vault's TVL net of amounts already
paid out, and it is recomputed at the start of each epoch. If the prior
epoch paid out its full cap, the next epoch's cap shrinks proportionally
against the remaining TVL. If the prior epoch under-used its cap, the
next epoch's cap is correspondingly larger.

When the queued withdraw requests for an epoch fit within that epoch's
cap, every request settles in its own epoch on the normal schedule. When
the queued demand for an epoch exceeds the cap, the surplus is paid in
the next epoch instead. A single request whose share amount is larger
than the remaining cap is sliced across consecutive epochs. See
[Withdrawal overflow](./overflow-concept) for how slicing is rendered.

Not every vault has a cap. A cap applies when the vault's on-chain
contract has a `withdrawalQueueThreshold` configured. The value, in basis
points, is read from the contract and surfaced to the frontend through
the Subgraph. Vaults without a configured threshold pay queued
withdrawals without a per-epoch ceiling.

When the cap is configured, the per-vault Withdrawal tab shows the cap
in two places. The queue card shows a cap progress bar for the open
epoch: how much of the current cap is already committed and how much
remains. The withdraw form projects, before you submit, which epoch each
portion of your request will settle in.

If your request fits within the open epoch's remaining cap, the form
shows a single settlement date. If your request exceeds the remaining
cap, the form shows one date per slice, mapped to the epoch that will
actually pay each portion. The total across slices reconstructs your
original request.

The cap and the cap-aware UI are tied to the vault. A cap on one vault
does not affect any other vault.
