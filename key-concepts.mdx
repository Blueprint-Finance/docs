Before you deposit, it helps to understand the handful of concepts that govern how Concrete vaults work, how your yield is tracked, and how the system protects depositors. Each term below maps directly to something you will see on the Concrete dashboard or encounter in the documentation.

A Concrete vault is an automated yield strategy container. You deposit a supported asset (such as WBTC or USDT) and the vault deploys that capital across a bundle of yield-generating strategies — lending markets, liquidity pools, restaking layers, and more. Each vault is built on the ERC-4626 tokenized vault standard, issuing ERC-20 shares that represent your proportional claim on the underlying assets.

    Each vault is dedicated to a single underlying asset and has its own set of strategies, risk parameters, and allocation caps. The vault contract enforces these parameters on-chain, meaning no party — including the vault's curator — can move capital outside the defined boundaries. 

    From your perspective, a vault behaves like a single product: deposit once, earn automatically, withdraw when you need to.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="eRate (Exchange Rate)">
    The exchange rate (eRate) tells you how much one vault share is worth in the vault's underlying asset, calculated as the vault's total assets divided by total share supply.

    When you deposit, your assets are converted into vault shares at the current eRate. You do not receive separate yield payments — instead, Concrete vault shares are rebasing via exchange rate changes, not via token rebasing. Your share count does not change unless you deposit, withdraw, or transfer. As the vault's strategies generate yield, totalAssets grows relative to totalSupply, and the eRate rises — meaning each share is worth more of the underlying asset than when you deposited.

    **Example:** If you deposit 100 USDT when the eRate is 1.00, you receive 100 vault shares. If the eRate later rises to 1.05, those same 100 shares are worth 105 USDT — a 5% gain with no additional action on your part.

    **How eRate relates to APY:** APY is the annualized expression of how the eRate changes over time. The eRate is the raw, point-in-time measurement of share value; APY translates the rate of change in eRate across a time window into a standardized yearly percentage, making it easier to compare vault performance against other yield sources. Concrete's APY Server computes these metrics from daily vault snapshots and serves them via a public API.

    The eRate can decrease if strategies report losses or when management and performance fees are accrued, both of which reduce the asset-per-share ratio. Under normal vault performance, however, the eRate trends upward over time and reflects the vault's cumulative net performance since inception.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="NAV (Net Asset Value)">
    NAV (Net Asset Value) is the total value of everything the vault holds across all of its positions and strategies at a given point in time, denominated in the vault's underlying asset (e.g., USDT, WBTC).

    Concrete's systems calculate NAV daily by snapshotting each strategy's position value — including idle balances, on-chain positions, and off-chain positions — and recording the total on-chain via the adjustTotalAssets call. The eRate (share price) is derived from this figure: NAV ÷ total shares outstanding = price per share (calculated after fee accrual).

    When the vault earns yield, NAV increases. Total share supply only changes when users deposit or withdraw, or when management and performance fees are accrued (both are paid by minting new shares to the fee recipient). Outside of fee accrual, each share becomes worth more as NAV grows.

    NAV is the vault's scoreboard — it tells you how much the entire pool is worth, and your shares represent your proportional piece of it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="ct[Asset] tokens">
    When you deposit into a Concrete vault, you receive **ct\[Asset\]** tokens in return. These are your vault shares — yield-bearing tokens that represent your proportional claim on the vault's total holdings.

    The token name reflects the underlying asset. For example:

    - Depositing USDT into the DeFi USDT vault gives you **ctDefiUSDT**
    - Depositing WBTC gives you **ctWBTC**

    The value of each ct\[Asset\] token, expressed in the underlying asset, increases as the vault earns yield — reflected in a rising eRate.

    To exit your position, you redeem your ct\[Asset\] shares and receive the underlying asset at the vault's share-to-asset exchange rate.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Active management">
    Concrete vaults do not passively hold assets in a single protocol. The vault continuously deploys capital between different yield-generating strategies — shifting toward better opportunities and pulling back from underperforming ones as market conditions change.

    This process includes:

    - Rebalancing across lending markets, liquidity pools, and restaking layers
    - Compounding rewards back into strategies
    - Rotating toward better risk-adjusted opportunities when markets shift

    Active management is what separates Concrete vaults from simpler, static yield products. Depositors who remain in the vault longer capture more rebalancing cycles and benefit from compounding that builds on itself over time.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Withdrawal queue">
    When you request a withdrawal from a Concrete vault that uses an asynchronous withdrawal queue, your request enters an epoch-based processing cycle rather than executing instantly. This is a deliberate design choice that protects all depositors. An instant withdrawal mechanism would force the vault to unwind positions quickly, potentially selling at unfavorable prices and reducing the NAV for everyone remaining in the vault.

    The withdrawal cycle has three stages: requests are queued in the current open epoch; the epoch closes on a configured cadence (daily, twice-weekly, or weekly, depending on the vault); the closed epoch is processed — at which point the share price is locked, shares are burned, and assets are reserved for claimants. You then claim your assets in a final on-chain step by returning to the vault page.

    Total time from request to claim depends on the vault's configured cadence and on whether strategy deallocation is required to source liquidity for the epoch. Please see each vault page for details on the 'Withdrawal Delay' ie the total processing time for withdrawals. 
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Curator">
    A **curator** is the entity responsible for managing strategy within a Concrete vault. Curators decide which protocols to allocate to, how to rebalance, and how to respond to market conditions — but they operate strictly within limits encoded in the vault contract.

    Key constraints on curators:

    - Curators can only interact with **whitelisted protocols** approved by the vault's configuration.
    - **Allocation caps** prevent over-concentration in any single strategy.
    - Curators manage strategy but not custody — withdrawals remain under protocol control, not curator discretion.
    - All curator addresses, fees, and performance are visible on-chain.

    This separation of duties is central to Concrete's security model. The vault contract enforces the rules; the curator operates within them. Trust is placed in the architecture, not in individual actors.
  </Accordion>
