## Cancelling a queued withdrawal


A queued withdrawal can be **cancelled by the requester at any time
before it is paid out**. Cancellation is a single on-chain action; once
confirmed, the request is removed from the queue and the user's share
balance is restored.

What the user is promised:

- Cancellation is available as long as the request is in the queue —
  whether it sits in the open epoch, in a closed-pending epoch, or
  split across multiple epochs as overflow slices.
- Cancelling a request removes **all of its slices** at once. A user
  cannot cancel just the overflow portion and keep the in-cap portion.
- Once cancelled, the underlying shares are immediately spendable: the
  user can leave them in the vault to keep earning yield or submit a
  fresh withdraw request to re-enter the queue at the current tail.
- A request that has already been settled (paid out on-chain) cannot be
  cancelled. From the user's side this is observable as the request
  having moved to a claimable or claimed state.

Cancellation does not impose a cooldown or fee at the protocol level.
Each cancel is a normal on-chain transaction with its own gas cost.

## Withdrawal disable




The vault processes withdrawals according to its standard flow.


A withdraw transaction submits a request that is queued to the next
applicable epoch.

## Epoch lifecycle


The vault progresses through repeating **epochs**. At any moment exactly
one epoch is *open* and accepting new requests; zero or more *closed-
pending* epochs sit between their cutoff and their payout.

The lifecycle is strictly sequential per epoch:

1. **Start.** The epoch opens and begins accepting requests.
2. **Cutoff.** This vault's cutoff runs on the cron schedule
   `—`. Requests submitted after the cutoff
   roll into the next epoch instead.
3. **Payout.** Closed-pending requests are settled on-chain and become
   claimable on the cron schedule `—`.
4. **Next epoch start.** A fresh epoch opens to receive new requests.

The cutoff and payout dates surfaced to users are computed from the
**current epoch's `startBlockTimestamp`** combined with the cron schedule —
never from wall-clock time alone. This guarantees the dates align with how
the contract actually advances.

## Withdrawal queue and delay


The vault settles withdrawals **asynchronously**. When a user submits a
withdraw request, the request is added to the open epoch's queue and is
paid out at that epoch's payout. The interval between submission and payout
is the *withdrawal delay*.

What the user is promised:

- The request is recorded on submission with a queue place (oldest = 1).
- The request is paid out at the **next payout** that falls after the
  epoch's cutoff, subject to any cap rules (see [Withdrawal caps](#withdrawal-caps)).
- Until paid, the request can be cancelled by the user; once cancelled it
  releases the user's share balance.

The duration of the delay is communicated to the user in three forms:

- The CMS field `withdrawalQueueDelay` — a human-readable summary
  (e.g. "up to 7 days") shown in the queue card prose.
- The CMS field `withdrawalQueueDescription` — optional rich-text override
  for vaults whose schedule needs extra explanation.
- The dynamically-computed next payout date — derived from the vault's
  withdrawal cron and current epoch (see [Epoch cycle](#epoch-lifecycle)).

The summary and description are editorial copy. The dynamic date is the
load-bearing value: it is what determines when assets actually become
claimable for a given request.

## Queue position numbering


Withdrawal requests are settled in **FIFO** order: the oldest unsettled
request is paid first, the next-oldest is paid next, and so on. The number
the UI shows next to a request is its position in that order — `1` means
"paid next", larger numbers mean "further back in line".


For vaults without withdrawal caps, every request queues into its native
epoch and the position is simply its FIFO place within that epoch's
queue. The number does not change once assigned (other than counting
down as earlier requests are paid).

## Schedule changes (RC config)


A vault's withdrawal cron schedule (cutoff time, payout time, cycle length)
can change over time — for example, moving from weekly to bi-weekly
payouts. To make these transitions predictable, every vault carries an
optional **release-candidate (RC) withdrawal config** with a `startingFrom`
timestamp.

What the user is promised:

- Epochs that start **before** `startingFrom` follow the previous
  (base) schedule end-to-end. Their cutoff and payout dates do not move.
- Epochs that start **at or after** `startingFrom` follow the new (RC)
  schedule.
- The schedule never changes mid-epoch: the boundary is always the start
  of an epoch, never inside one.



A "Learn more" link can be attached via `withdrawalRcLearnMoreLink` to
explain a specific schedule change to users; it is shown in the queue card
when an RC config is active or imminent for the vault.
