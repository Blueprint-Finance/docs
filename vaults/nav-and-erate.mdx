Two concepts explain how yield accumulates in a Concrete vault and why your `ct[Asset]` shares are worth more over time: **NAV** (Net Asset Value) and the **exchange rate**. NAV measures the total value held by the vault; the exchange rate is the per-share value derived from it.

## NAV: the vault's total value

NAV stands for **Net Asset Value**. It is the total value of everything the vault currently holds across all of its positions and strategies, denominated in the vault's underlying asset (for example, USDT for a USDT vault, WBTC for a WBTC vault). When the vault's strategies generate yield, NAV increases. When strategies report losses, NAV decreases. 

Your shares represent a proportional piece of the vault's NAV. For example, if a USDT vault holds 1,100,000 USDT in total and there are 1,000,000 shares outstanding, each share is redeemable for 1.10 USDT.

## **Exchange rate: what one vault share is worth**

The **exchange rate** tells you how much one `ct[Asset]` vault share is worth in the underlying asset — for example, how many USDT one share of a USDT vault can be redeemed for. The exchange rate is calculated as:

```text
exchange rate = total vault assets ÷ total shares outstanding
```

**A simple example:** Ten depositors each deposit 100 USDT into a vault. The vault holds 1,000 USDT total, and each depositor holds a 1/10 share. The vault's strategies earn yield and the total grows to 1,100 USDT. Nobody added funds. Nobody received extra shares. But each 1/10 share is now worth 110 USDT. The exchange rate has moved from 1.00 to 1.10 — each share is redeemable for 10% more of the underlying asset than at the start. When you withdraw, your `ct[Asset]` shares are redeemed against the exchange rate that applies at settlement. For vaults using an asynchronous withdrawal queue (the most common configuration), this is the exchange rate locked at the time the epoch containing your request is processed.

## How daily NAV updates work

Concrete Earn V2 uses an automated three-party model to update vault NAV daily. No single party can unilaterally change the vault's reported value.

The three parties are:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Transaction Proposer">
    An automation bot that tracks strategist wallet balances and on-chain positions, computes the current portfolio value, and proposes an update to the vault contract. The proposer does not execute the transaction — it submits the proposal and requests validation from an independent signer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Independent Signer">
    A separate policy engine that does not trust the proposer. It independently re-calculates the expected exchange rate from its own data sources, validates the structural and business-logic correctness of the proposed update, simulates the transaction, and only then countersigns.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Smart Contract Safeguards">
    The vault's underlying strategy contract enforces on-chain bounds on every accounting update — including a maximum allowed change per update (derived from the vault's target annualized yield), a minimum cooldown between updates, and a validity window after which operations dependent on accounting will revert. Updates exceeding these bounds auto-pause the strategy and require administrator intervention to resume.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Together, these layers replace manual bookkeeping with daily, verified accounting that runs on a scheduled lifecycle without manual oversight.

## Frequently asked questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How often is NAV updated?">
    Concrete's automation updates vault NAV daily on a scheduled lifecycle. Each update flows through the three-party model above before being applied to the vault.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How does NAV relate to TVL?">
    TVL (Total Value Locked) is the figure displayed in the Concrete app for the size of a vault, and it is derived from the vault's NAV. NAV is the on-chain accounting measure of total assets in the underlying asset; TVL is the same value expressed for display purposes (often in a USD equivalent). When NAV rises or falls, the TVL shown in the app reflects the change once the next daily update is applied.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How does the exchange rate relate to APY?">
    APY (Annual Percentage Yield) is an annualized expression of how the exchange rate changes over time. The exchange rate is the raw, point-in-time measurement of share value; APY translates the rate of change in the exchange rate across a measurement window into a standardized yearly percentage, making it easier to compare vault performance against other yield sources. The APY shown in the Concrete app is computed from daily vault snapshots.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
